# Computational Checks

Initial local validation: October 26, 2026, Python 3.13.

- `python -m pytest -q`: 43 tests passed.
- Default experiment completed: 900 independent datasets, 3,600 estimator
  evaluations, 60 scenario/window/method summaries, and 60 paired comparisons.
- Three figures rendered and inspected. They are generated by the experiment
  code, not edited charts or copied illustrations.
- The undersampled case has 32 assets and 24 observations. Singular sample
  covariance is allowed; allocation has a small, documented numerical ridge.
- The no-look-ahead test changes future returns and confirms that current and
  earlier allocations and forecasts stay unchanged.
- Drift accounting is checked against a two-asset example: after +10% and -10%
  returns, equal initial weights become 55%/45%. Rebalancing back to 50%/50%
  trades 10% of NAV, not zero.
- Costs include the initial investment from cash. Changing costs does not change
  the allocation policy but decreases wealth on the same path.
- A 200,000-draw t-noise check verifies the generator's covariance scaling within
  a fixed numerical tolerance. This is a simulation check, not a proof.

## Observations

With weak factors and 64 observations, sample and RMT relative covariance errors
average 0.601 and 0.342. Their variance/oracle ratios are 1.390 and 1.190.
With changing covariance and 160 observations, the sample estimate's ratio is
1.646 versus RMT's 1.685. The paired RMT-minus-sample difference is 0.040,
with exploratory bootstrap interval [0.011, 0.068].

The diagonal baseline performs well when assets are truly independent, but
substantially understates portfolio risk in the correlated scenarios. Heavy
tails and stale observations remain important failure cases.

There is no claim of historical trading performance. The single rolling path
has losses for all policies, and those losses are retained in the output.
Further real-data validation and broader parameter/seed studies remain open.
